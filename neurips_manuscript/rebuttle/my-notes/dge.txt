Macrophage islands (SPP1, GPNMB)

- Lung-specific, and exactly this gene pair: "Gpnmb and Spp1 mark a conserved macrophage injury response... in the lung" (JCI Insight). This isn't pan-cancer generic — it's the same two genes, together, specifically in lung tissue, independent of the NSCLC source paper entirely.
- Lung cancer specific: "The Significance of SPP1 in Lung Cancers and Its Impact as a Marker for Protumor Tumor-Associated Macrophages" (PMC10136569) — SPP1+ TAMs linked to poor prognosis and chemo resistance specifically in lung adenocarcinoma.
- GPNMB mechanism: independently confirmed as "a hallmark of lipid-associated macrophages... GPNMB+ TAMs are enriched in pathways of matrix degradation, angiogenesis, and lipid metabolism" — matches the source paper's own description ("lipid-laden marker... typical of tissue-repair and pro-tumoral macrophages") almost verbatim, from a completely separate literature base.

This is now about as solid as a two-gene signature gets: same genes, same direction, confirmed in lung tissue specifically, by work unconnected to your source dataset.

Desmoplastic stroma (COL14A1, IGF1, CXCL12)

- COL14A1: confirmed as a marker of inflammatory CAFs (iCAFs) specifically — "the most upregulated collagen compared to all other CAF subtypes" in that subtype — and separately appears in a 36-protein desmoplastic-stroma signature paper. Real, independent confirmation.
- CXCL12: very well established broadly as a CAF-secreted factor across multiple cancer types (pancreatic, gastric) — "CXCL12 alone is enough to reprogram normal fibroblasts into cancer-associated fibroblasts." Strong, cross-tumor-type support, though not lung-specific in what surfaced.
- IGF1: the weakest of the three here — didn't independently surface as a headline CAF marker in general search (results pointed to adjacent pathways like TGF-β/IGFBP7 instead). It's still named directly by the NSCLC source paper itself, just not as strongly corroborated elsewhere as the other two.
