LESSONS FOR FUTURE — scProto / NeurIPS 2026 rebuttal
=====================================================
Written Aug 2026, after the discussion period closed.
Sections marked [orig] are my own observations from the first draft.


0. THE COSTLIEST MISTAKE HAPPENED BEFORE SUBMISSION
---------------------------------------------------
The paper shipped without ablations.

All three reviewers AND the AC independently flagged this. The AC made it the
headline of the meta-review: "all reviewers identify the lack of ablations as a
major weakness... the missing controls and ablations appear substantial."

Those experiments took me three weeks during the rebuttal. They were runnable
all along. If they had been in the submission, the reviews would have started
1-2 points higher, and the whole rebuttal would have been a different game —
converting borderline-accepts instead of arguing up from borderline-rejects.

RULE: a paper claiming a multi-term objective ships with leave-one-term-out
ablations. No exceptions. Same for significance tests on the headline tables.

Second version of the same mistake: my headline metric (modularity) was also my
training objective AND my early-stopping criterion. Two reviewers independently
called circularity. A self-scoring metric needs a held-out protocol IN THE
PAPER, not invented under fire.


1. WHAT I ACTUALLY DID WELL — KEEP DOING THIS
----------------------------------------------
- Reported nulls and unfavourable results as plainly as the wins. Reviewer e9Ho
  said so explicitly: "I appreciate that the null and unfavourable results were
  reported as plainly as the rest." Reviewers almost never say this. It is the
  most valuable thing I produced and it carries beyond this paper.
- Conceded real errors: l.229 overclaim, l.150-152 mechanism, "never reverse",
  the nassoc-on-purity claim that didn't hold.
- Enormous experimental throughput in 3 weeks — more than any of the 7
  comparison papers I studied, including the accepted ones.
- Mechanistic thinking, not just benchmark-stacking. "The objective adds
  wherever the graph carries signal the reconstruction likelihood does not" is
  a falsifiable claim about WHEN the method helps — and it held, including
  where it predicted I would stop winning.
- Structured the AC-facing comment around the AC's own points, in their order.


2. MY MAIN WEAKNESS
--------------------
I treated the rebuttal as an EVIDENCE-PRODUCTION problem when it was an
ARGUMENT problem.

Evidence accumulated; the thesis never consolidated. Symptoms:
- Best conceptual sentence appeared ONCE, as a §2 subheading, in one reviewer's
  thread, posted the day after that reviewer stopped reading. The fuller version
  (in ac-comment.md) was never posted at all.
- No common-response post. 4 of the 7 papers I studied used one — it is how an
  AC sees a single argument instead of three dense threads.
- The claim moved under pressure ("modularity wasn't my claim", "rare cells were
  a proxy") instead of being stated once upfront and held.
- Responses got LONGER over the rounds, not sharper.

Calibration so I don't over-blame myself: this was real but probably not
decisive. Starting position dominates. A rebuttal amplifies where you already
are — in all 7 cases I studied, no paper moved from reject to accept on the
strength of a response. The accepted ones were already at the line.


3. THE LOOP, REVISED
---------------------
[orig] My loop was:
  read reviews -> decide what to answer/run -> experiment plan -> run ->
  results don't look good -> think of NEW experiments -> run again ->
  looks fine -> write

[orig] What I skipped: interpreting the results myself before drafting.

Two changes:

(a) MOVE INTERPRETATION BEFORE THE EXPERIMENT.
    Before running anything, write down: "if this comes back X it supports the
    claim; if it comes back Y the claim has to change to Z."
    Then an unfavourable result is INFORMATION, not a crisis — and the reflex
    to run something else disappears. This single rule also prevents the
    overclaiming pattern that cost me four separate concessions.

(b) WRITE THE ONE-SENTENCE THESIS FIRST, THEN THE TABLES.
    Every strong response I studied had one. Mine had it sometimes; the
    responses where it was missing read as number dumps.

Revised loop:
  read reviews
  -> TRIAGE each point by type (section 4)
  -> for each: state in advance what would support / falsify
  -> run only what the triage says needs running
  -> interpret MYSELF: what does this mean for the claim?
  -> write the thesis sentence
  -> draft myself (rough is fine)
  -> ask Claude to sharpen / compress / attack


4. TRIAGE — FOUR KINDS OF REVIEWER OBJECTION
---------------------------------------------
I defaulted to "run an experiment" for all four. They need different responses.

(1) FACTUAL ERROR / MISREADING
    -> One-sentence correction. Concede immediately and completely.
    Example: l.229 overclaim. I conceded — correct.

(2) GENUINE EVIDENTIAL GAP
    -> Run the experiment.
    Example: the ablations. Correct.

(3) VALUE DISAGREEMENT about what should be measured
    -> A reframe, not data.
    Example: e9Ho on scIB metrics. I wrote the argument AND ran kBET/iLISI.
    The argument was the right answer; the metrics were optional.

(4) PHILOSOPHICAL OBJECTION with no fully satisfying answer
    -> Concede the limit and scope the claim. Do not keep adding protocols.
    Example: F5RB on held-out-edge independence. Any held-out test within one
    dataset shares the generating process. I kept adding evidence to answer
    something partly unfalsifiable.

CONCEDE FIRST, THEN RE-RUN. scRatio (ICML 2026) was accused of unfair baseline
tuning, replied "Apologies... we overlooked tuning the SB variance", and re-ran
with a proper validation split. BOTH reviewers cited that as the decisive
moment. I argued first on Harmony ("dimensionality bug") and had to be pushed
into the fairer comparison. Arguing first costs the credit.


5. PLACEMENT AND TIMING
------------------------
- STRONGEST EVIDENCE GOES IN THE FIRST RESPONSE, NOT THE LAST.
  My scVI-backbone result — the single best thing I produced, and the one that
  answers the AC's OWN question — landed Aug 4, after every reviewer's final
  comment. Nobody rebutted it because nobody read it.

- PUT THE BEST SENTENCE WHERE IT GETS READ: abstract, first line of the AC
  comment, first line of each reviewer reply. Not a subheading in §2 of the
  last post.

- WRITE A COMMON-RESPONSE POST for concerns shared by 2+ reviewers.

- WRITE AN AC-FACING SUMMARY at the end, organised in the AC's own order.
  (I did this — keep it.)

- NUDGE SILENT REVIEWERS. Every accepted paper I studied did this, some twice.


6. WHAT DECIDES THE OUTCOME (from 7 OpenReview threads)
--------------------------------------------------------
Papers studied: FlatVI (ICLR'25 reject / ICML'25 spotlight — same paper),
CFGen (ICLR'25), scRatio (ICML'26), NicheFlow (NeurIPS'25),
FrameShield (NeurIPS'25), SGD-shortcut (ICLR'26).

THE RULE THAT EXPLAINS ALL SEVEN: acceptance tracks whether at least ONE
reviewer affirmatively advocates. Not scores, not movement, not effort.
- The only rejection (FlatVI at ICLR) had the HIGHEST raw scores (6,6,5,5) and
  no advocate — its top score said "would not mind if paper is rejected".
- The same paper at ICML, with two weak-rejects and a silent reviewer, was a
  SPOTLIGHT three months later.
- SGD-shortcut was accepted at 4/6/8 with ZERO score movement and a reviewer
  who never replied — because one reviewer wrote "I recommend acceptance".
- scRatio's AC: "I am basing this meta-review on the substance of the reviews
  and ensuing discussions, rather than directly on the current scores...
  reviewer U3Wf has advocated for publication."

Corollaries:
- Silent or hostile reviewers did not block acceptance in 4 of 6 accepts.
- EVERY accepted paper had results that came back against them. Ties and nulls
  are not disqualifying.
- Volume is not the lever. NicheFlow refused the held-out evaluation, declined
  three requested baselines, never revised the PDF — and was accepted.
- A rejection is not a verdict on the science. FlatVI: rejected at ICLR with an
  AC note "I encourage the authors to... submit again", spotlight at ICML.


7. WORKING WITH AI ON REBUTTALS
--------------------------------
[orig] Always, before asking Claude: clarify for myself exactly what I want to
say and in what detail. Even if the detail is a lot, it is easier than asking
for a million changes after Claude has drafted it.

This is measured, not just intuition. From "Defend: Automated Rebuttals for
Peer Review with Minimal Author Guidance" (arXiv 2603.27360):

    approach                          factual correctness   strength of refutation
    direct end-to-end LLM generation        0.45                  0.12
    segment-wise generation                 0.58                  0.29
    author-guided structured pipeline       0.74                  0.76

Strength of refutation collapses 6x when the LLM generates from raw material
instead of author-supplied structure. Their conclusion: "rebuttal writing is
not a simple generative task"; "LLMs often struggle to perform targeted
refutation and maintain accurate factual grounding."

Their pipeline is finer than mine and the extra step is the triage in §4:
  segment review -> is this a real deficiency? -> what KIND of error?
  -> choose response strategy -> only then generate.

DIVISION OF LABOUR THAT WORKS:
  I own: the claim, the interpretation, what each result means.
  Claude owns: structure, compression, adversarial pressure.

  Good prompt: "here is what I think this result shows and why — attack it"
  Bad prompt: "here are the numbers, write something"
  The first gets a stress-tested argument. The second gets fluent prose with
  no spine.

USE IT EARLIER. Reading the submitted paper in June, an adversarial pass would
have flagged the circularity of modularity, the abstract/Table-1 mismatch, and
the missing ablations. None of that needed the reviews to become visible.
Cheapest, highest-leverage hour available.


8. PRE-SUBMISSION CHECKLIST FOR NEXT TIME
------------------------------------------
[ ] Leave-one-term-out ablation on EVERY dataset, in the main text
[ ] Significance tests on every headline table
[ ] Headline metric is NOT the training objective or the stopping criterion
    (or a held-out protocol is in the paper)
[ ] Every abstract claim checked line-by-line against the tables
[ ] Claims stated as the honest result (trade-off if it's a trade-off) — the
    honest framing is usually STRONGER as well as true
[ ] Baselines run at their own recommended configurations, not only mine
[ ] Best conceptual sentence appears in the abstract
[ ] Someone adversarial reads it and tries to reject it before submission
[ ] Primary area chosen to draw reviewers who value the problem, not a novelty
    bar the method can't clear


=========================================================================
9. THE CORRECT LOOP  <<< THE ONE THING TO REREAD BEFORE THE NEXT REBUTTAL
=========================================================================

  STEP 0 — READ EVERYTHING ONCE, WRITE NOTHING
    Read all reviews + meta-review. Do not open a doc. Do not run anything.
    The AC's meta-review is the priority list — it says what would change
    the AC's mind. Answer that first, in the AC's own order.

  STEP 1 — TRIAGE EVERY POINT BY TYPE  (do this before any experiment)
    For each reviewer point, label it:
      (1) factual error / misreading  -> one-sentence correction, concede now
      (2) genuine evidential gap      -> run the experiment
      (3) value disagreement          -> reframe, not data
      (4) unfalsifiable objection     -> concede the limit, scope the claim
    Only type (2) becomes an experiment. This is the step I skipped.

  STEP 2 — WRITE THE THESIS SENTENCE, BEFORE ANY RESULTS EXIST
    One sentence: what is true about my method, stated honestly, including
    its limits. If it's a trade-off, say trade-off.
    Everything downstream is arranged around this sentence.

  STEP 3 — PRE-REGISTER EACH EXPERIMENT
    Before running: "if X, the claim holds; if Y, the claim becomes Z."
    Write Y down. Committing to Y in advance is what stops the
    result-looks-bad -> run-something-else loop.

  STEP 4 — RUN
    Only what Step 1 marked as type (2). Nothing else.

  STEP 5 — INTERPRET MYSELF  (no AI yet)
    For each result: does this support, narrow, or break the thesis?
    If it narrows it -> narrow the thesis NOW, in my own words.
    Do not proceed until I can say out loud what every table means.

  STEP 6 — DRAFT MYSELF, ROUGH
    Bullet points are fine. But the claim, the interpretation and the
    ordering must be mine.

  STEP 7 — AI: SHARPEN, COMPRESS, ATTACK
    "Here is what I think this shows and why — attack it."
    Never: "here are the numbers, write something."
    Then have it compress and check every claim against every table.

  STEP 8 — PLACE IT PROPERLY
    - thesis sentence = FIRST line of every reply, not a subheading
    - strongest evidence = FIRST response, not the last
    - shared concerns    = one common-response post
    - AC summary         = organised in the AC's own order
    - nudge silent reviewers, twice if needed

  STEP 9 — POST EARLY, THEN ITERATE
    Engage in round 1 so reviewers have time to respond and reconsider.
    Evidence posted after a reviewer's last comment is evidence nobody read.

  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE TWO SENTENCES THAT MATTER MOST:

    Decide what the result MEANS before you decide what to write.
    Decide what would FALSIFY the claim before you run the experiment.
  ---------------------------------------------------------------------
