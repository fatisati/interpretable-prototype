\begin{abstract}
Resolving which cell states are present in a tissue, and which cells occupy them, is essential for characterizing cellular heterogeneity in development and disease. Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics now profile many donors and tissue sections at once, but each cell is too sparse to analyse on its own, and cells are therefore aggregated into representative profiles termed metacells. Current methods form these metacells from proximity in observed expression alone, so a metacell follows whatever dominates that expression---batch or abundance---rather than the cell state itself. A state that is rare in every batch, though common across the experiment, therefore cannot be formed at all. A cell's spatial niche can also modulate its expression, but that variation is small beside cell-type differences and is absorbed into them. Adding spatial context to the affinity overcorrects, splitting one state across neighbourhoods even where the niche leaves no signal in expression. We introduce scProto, a prototype-based deep clustering model that learns metacells across an entire experiment rather than within each batch. Instead of clustering the affinity graph directly, scProto uses it as a training objective, learning a batch-corrected representation of expression with a conditional variational autoencoder, jointly with a soft assignment of cells to prototypes. Cells strongly connected in the graph are driven to share prototype assignments, while a graph-partitioning criterion keeps each group internally dense and separated from the others. Assignments are computed from expression alone---spatial information enters only through the affinity graph---so a niche can separate cells only to the extent that it changes their transcriptome. Across single-cell and spatial transcriptomics datasets, scProto recovers both states that no single batch contains enough cells to define and states associated with a cell's spatial niche, improving batch mixing and rare-population recovery while preserving community structure.
\end{abstract}
