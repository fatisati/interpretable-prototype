We thank the Area Chair and the reviewers for the detailed feedback, which has improved the paper. scProto builds metacells across batches by learning shared prototypes on a batch-conditioned VAE, under an objective aligning each cell's assignment to a cell–cell affinity graph. Below is a summary of the outcomes and additions.

**Which components drive the gains.** This was the meta-review's central question, so we ran the full 8-arm leave-one-term-out ablation on all three datasets, with a paired per-batch test on each arm. The affinity term is the largest single effect in the study, on every dataset: removing it collapses modularity — how well metacells preserve the graph's communities — on 16 of 16 Lung batches (p=3.1e-5). The usage term keeps prototypes from collapsing onto a fraction of their capacity; reconstruction changes what prototypes decode to but not which cells they group, its gradient stopped before the encoder. Weight and K sweeps show no setting to be delicate.

**Baseline completeness and configuration.** That leaves whether the gains survive stronger comparison. The two-step baselines are now complete — scVI, Harmony, BBKNN, ComBat and SuperCell with SEACells or Leiden on top, plus Metacell-2, whose K follows its own policy and is shown for reference — and since the reviewers asked whether a shared setting favoured us, we ran each twice: on a common configuration, and at its own. scProto leads on community structure in both. On rare cells it leads under the common configuration, significantly against every tested baseline on Pancreas and Lung, while at the baselines' own settings part of that comparison levels out, for a reason we return to below.

**Cross-batch alignment and Stage 2.** Beneath both sits where alignment originates. The Area Chair's reading is correct and we have adopted it: alignment comes from Stage 1, while the Stage-2 reconstruction path does not update the encoder through the prototype assignments; lines 150–152 are corrected. What Stage 2 adds is different. Stage 1's objective is summed over cells, so abundant states dominate and a sparse population can be absorbed into a denser neighbour, after which no downstream clusterer recovers a separation the embedding no longer holds. Stage 2 keeps training the encoder against affinity graphs built within each batch, where technical variation largely cancels, while prototypes are shared across batches — so a rare state too sparse to hold its own in one batch can accumulate into a shared prototype. SEACells and Leiden on the frozen Stage-1 embedding reach scProto on no dataset.

**Modularity and the baseline clusterer.** The remaining concern, which the Area Chair singled out, was whether the community-structure result is measured independently, modularity being part of what scProto trains on. K-means is replaced throughout by Leiden and SEACells, and modularity is no longer scored on the graph the model trained on: we mask 20% of edges before training and score only the hidden ones, and separately hold out 20% of cells from graph construction and training, scoring them through a frozen encoder. scProto leads under both.

We also tested whether the metacells support discovery of niche-correlated transcriptional programs, using MetaQ's protocol with ground truth from the differential expression of the real cells in each (cell type, niche) pair. On six pairs scProto isolates the pair and recovers the correct genes where neither baseline can, two matching independently described biology. Limited in scope, but indicative of the method's potential here.

Read together, these follow from one idea. A VAE does place similar cells near one another, but only as a by-product of fitting each profile: similarity there is whatever reconstruction error implies, dominated by the states with the most cells, and cannot be told to be density-corrected or defined by spatial context instead. scProto's affinity term supplies that, making group membership a target rather than a by-product. It also accounts for where the margin narrows. A likelihood already modelling what a metric measures leaves the term little to add there. And a graph partitioner can match modularity on the graph it partitions, but a partition only labels the cells it was given and models no expression: batch correction must come from whatever produced the graph, and a cell outside it cannot be placed. scProto learns expression-to-assignment, which lets one objective preserve cell-type identity, mix batches and extend to held-out cells.

That combination holds across three scRNA-seq datasets, two spatial platforms and two backbones, the same objective taking either a transcriptomic or a spatially-aware graph. All experiments above, with the corrections to lines 150–152 and 229 and the abstract, will be in the revised manuscript.
