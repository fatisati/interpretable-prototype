\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

Most existing methods fall into one of two camps: affinity-based methods preserve community structure but remain restricted to within-batch aggregation, limiting coverage of rare populations; reconstruction-based models align cells across batches but do not preserve this structure, allowing rare cell states to be absorbed into denser regions of the latent space. scProto addresses both by combining a community-preserving objective with reconstruction-based cross-batch alignment.

The affinity framework extends naturally to spatial settings: defining affinity from spatially aggregated features enables scProto to capture niche-correlated transcriptional programs while the reconstruction objective preserves cell-type identity. Directions for future work include more principled spatial affinities based on ligand--receptor interactions or local cell-type composition, and methods that automatically determine $K$ rather than requiring it to be fixed in advance.
