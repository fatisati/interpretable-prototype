
Niche-Aware Metacells Reveal Microenvironment-DrivenTranscriptional Programs in Spatial Transcriptomics
Nature Methods — Paper Structure &amp; Required Results Blueprint
The Story (One-Paragraph Pitch)
Metacell methods compress single-cell data by grouping transcriptomically similar cells — but cell state is shaped by both intrinsic identity and local microenvironment. A macrophage in a tumor core and a macrophage at the tumor-stroma boundary may share nearly identical gene expression profiles, yet harbor distinct functional programs driven by the different cell types surrounding them. Because existing metacell methods define similarity from a cell's own gene expression alone, these niche-driven programs are averaged away when such cells are merged. We introduce niche-aware metacells: by defining cell–cell affinity from the COVET embedding — a second-order statistic that encodes the compositional fingerprint of each cell's spatial neighborhood (which cell types co-occur nearby, and in what proportion) — we guide aggregation toward cells sharing similar microenvironmental context. Plugging this affinity into scProto yields metacells that preserve both cell-type identity and niche structure, revealing transcriptional programs that transcriptomics-only methods cannot recover.

Introduction (4 Paragraphs)
Paragraph 1 — Metacells as a tool
Metacells compress noisy scRNA-seq into stable cell states.
Standard approach: aggregate by transcriptomic similarity.
Used widely for denoising, atlas building, downstream analysis.
Paragraph 2 — The microenvironment shapes cell state
Spatial transcriptomics reveals that identical cell types in different tissue contexts express different programs.
Cite: tumor microenvironment immune exclusion, cortical layer specialization, fibroblast activation at boundaries.
This is established biology. Current methods ignore it entirely.
Paragraph 3 — The gap
Existing metacell methods (MetaCell, SEACells, MetaQ, SuperSpot) define similarity from transcriptomics or spatial proximity alone. Neither captures niche composition.
Two cells with the same transcriptome but different neighbors are merged; niche-driven programs are diluted.
Concretely: a metacell spanning tumor core and tumor margin macrophages will show no differential expression between niches even if real programs exist.
Paragraph 4 — What you do + contributions
COVET affinity: second-order neighborhood statistic encodes niche composition as a matrix fingerprint; cells in similar microenvironments → similar affinity regardless of their own transcriptome.
scProto framework: plug-in affinity enables niche-aware grouping with reconstruction anchor (preserves cell-type identity). Cite Hashemi et al. NeurIPS 2026.
Result: niche-aware metacells reveal niche-correlated transcriptional programs invisible to existing approaches.
Available as open-source tool.

Discussion (1 page)
What niche-aware metacells enable that wasn't possible before.
When to use niche vs transcriptomic affinity (or both — concat).
Limitations:
COVET requires k ≥ 3×n_pcs cells per neighborhood; sparse spatial data degrades covariance estimation.
K (number of metacells) must be set in advance.
Single-slide COVET does not benefit from cross-batch alignment.
Future directions:
3D spatial data (already partially supported in code — z-axis scaling).
Ligand–receptor-informed affinity.
Integration with single-cell foundation model embeddings (cite scConcept, Richter et al. NMI 2025).
